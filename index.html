01 • Introduction

What Is a Solar Panel?

A solar panel—or photovoltaic (PV) module—is an electronic product that converts sunlight directly into electrical energy through the photovoltaic effect.

Why learn about solar panels?

Solar technology is used in homes, schools, farms, streetlights, charging stations, and remote facilities. Preparing a system combines electronics knowledge, practical skills, planning, and safety.

Learning objectives

  • Explain how photovoltaic cells generate electricity.
  • Identify solar-system components and their functions.
  • Describe tools, materials, and preparation steps.
  • Explain safe assembly, testing, and troubleshooting.
  • Calculate basic energy and power requirements.
  • Appreciate renewable energy and sustainability.
Key idea: A solar panel needs light—not heat—to generate electricity. Excessive cell temperature can reduce its output.

03 • Understand

How a Solar Panel Works

Follow the energy from sunlight to a useful electrical load.

Photovoltaic modules receive sunlight and generate DC electricity.
1

Sunlight absorption

Photons transfer energy to electrons in semiconductor cells, commonly made from silicon.

2

Charge separation

An internal electric field helps separate charges and creates electrical potential.

3

Electricity generation

When connected to a circuit, electrons flow and produce direct current (DC).

4

Energy use or storage

Power can operate compatible DC loads, charge a battery through a controller, or be converted to AC by an inverter.

04 • Compare

Types of Solar Panels & Systems

Different technologies and system configurations suit different applications.

Monocrystalline

Single-crystal silicon; typically dark in appearance and efficient in limited space.

Polycrystalline

Multiple silicon crystals; commonly blue in appearance and generally lower efficiency than many mono panels.

Thin-film / Flexible

Thin semiconductor layers; some designs are lightweight or flexible, depending on construction.

Off-grid

Independent of utility power; commonly uses panels, charge controller, battery, and possibly inverter.

Remote locations

Grid-tied

Connected to the utility through an approved inverter and interconnection equipment. Typical systems shut down during grid outages unless designed otherwise.

Homes / buildings

Hybrid

Combines solar, battery storage, and utility connection; backup operation depends on equipment and design.

Flexible energy

Assembly sequence: review each step

Step 1 — Inspect and position the panel

Inspect glass, frame, junction box, leads, and connectors. Place the module on a stable support and avoid shading where possible.

Step 2 — Read specifications and confirm compatibility

Check Voc, Isc, Vmp, Imp, battery chemistry/voltage, controller PV limits, current ratings, and load requirements.

Step 3 — Connect the battery to the controller (where required)

Many controllers require battery connection first to recognize system voltage. Follow the exact controller manual, including disconnect sequence.

Step 4 — Connect PV input and verify polarity

Use appropriate cables/connectors. Confirm positive and negative, allowable PV voltage, and protective devices before connecting.

Step 5 — Connect a compatible load

Use controller load terminals only if rated for the load; otherwise use a properly designed and protected circuit.

Step 6 — Inspect, test, and document

Check wiring, insulation, terminals, and protection. Conduct only approved measurements with correctly rated instruments, then record readings and observations.

06 • Circuit concepts

Series and Parallel Connections

Connecting panels changes the electrical output. The design must remain within controller and protection ratings.

Series connection

Panel A (+) → Panel B (−)
Voltage adds • Current stays similar

For two matched panels operating at 12 V and 5 A each: total operating voltage ≈ 24 V, current ≈ 5 A, power ≈ 120 W.

Parallel connection

Positive to positive
Negative to negative
Current adds • Voltage stays similar

For two matched panels operating at 12 V and 5 A each: voltage ≈ 12 V, current ≈ 10 A, power ≈ 120 W.

Design caution: Use matched modules where appropriate and calculate cold-weather Voc, Isc, wiring, and protection. Never exceed the controller's maximum PV input voltage/current.

08 • Quality check

Testing, Troubleshooting & Maintenance

Testing verifies operation; troubleshooting finds causes; maintenance helps preserve performance.

Multimeter testing

  1. Use a meter with suitable DC voltage rating.
  2. For open-circuit voltage, measure across panel terminals with no load, using safe leads and setup.
  3. Compare the reading with specifications and conditions.
  4. Measure operating voltage only in a suitable connected circuit.
  5. Current measurement requires a correctly rated meter in series or a suitable clamp meter.
Never: Put a meter in current mode directly across a battery or voltage source—it can create a short circuit.

09 • Safety first

Safety Rules for Solar Work

Safety is part of product quality—not an optional step.

⚡ Electrical

  • Check polarity and ratings.
  • Never short panel or battery terminals.
  • Use DC-rated protection.
  • Isolate equipment using approved procedures.

🔋 Battery

  • Use compatible charging settings.
  • Prevent short circuits.
  • Follow chemistry-specific guidance.
  • Do not use damaged batteries.

🦺 Work area

  • Keep the bench dry and organized.
  • Use appropriate PPE.
  • Do not handle cracked panels.
  • Rooftop work requires trained professionals and fall protection.
Grid-connected systems: Require qualified professionals, approved utility interconnection, permits/inspection as applicable, grounding/bonding, and appropriate protection. In the Philippines, follow applicable Philippine electrical standards and utility requirements.

Wrap-up

What Have You Learned?

A solar product is more than a panel: it is a planned, connected, tested, and maintained system.

Remember the process: Plan → Select → Inspect → Prepare → Assemble → Test → Document → Maintain.

Core principle: A successful electronic product must be safe, functional, reliable, properly tested, and appropriate for its intended application.

Further reading: U.S. Department of Energy — Solar Photovoltaic Technology Basics and System Design Basics; Philippine Bureau of Philippine Standards (DTI-BPS) — applicable Philippine National Standards for PV systems. Always consult current manufacturer documentation and local electrical requirements.

📊 How to Synchronize Assessment & Live Stats to Google Sheets

Follow these quick steps to link quiz submissions AND real-time views/likes to your Google Sheet using Google Apps Script:

  1. Open Google Sheets and create a new spreadsheet.
  2. In the first tab, name it Quiz Submissions and add these exact headers in row 1:
    Timestamp | Full Name | Grade Level | Section | Score | Total Questions | Percentage
  3. Create a second tab by clicking the ➕ icon at the bottom and name it exactly: Stats
  4. In the Stats tab, set cell A1 to Views and cell B1 to Likes. Set A2 to 0 and B2 to 0.
  5. Click on Extensions → Apps Script.
  6. Delete any existing code in the editor and paste the complete Google Apps Script code below:
    function doGet(e) { var ss = SpreadsheetApp.getActiveSpreadsheet(); var statsSheet = ss.getSheetByName("Stats"); if (!statsSheet) { statsSheet = ss.insertSheet("Stats"); statsSheet.getRange("A1").setValue("Views"); statsSheet.getRange("B1").setValue("Likes"); statsSheet.getRange("A2").setValue(0); statsSheet.getRange("B2").setValue(0); } var action = e && e.parameter ? e.parameter.action : "get"; var views = parseInt(statsSheet.getRange("A2").getValue()) || 0; var likes = parseInt(statsSheet.getRange("B2").getValue()) || 0; if (action === "visit") { views += 1; statsSheet.getRange("A2").setValue(views); } else if (action === "like") { likes += 1; statsSheet.getRange("B2").setValue(likes); } else if (action === "unlike") { likes = Math.max(0, likes - 1); statsSheet.getRange("B2").setValue(likes); } var output = JSON.stringify({ views: views, likes: likes }); return ContentService.createTextOutput(output).setMimeType(ContentService.MimeType.JSON); } function doPost(e) { try { var ss = SpreadsheetApp.getActiveSpreadsheet(); var sheet = ss.getSheetByName("Quiz Submissions") || ss.getSheets()[0]; var data = JSON.parse(e.postData.contents); sheet.appendRow([ data.timestamp || new Date(), data.name, data.grade, data.section, data.score, data.total, data.percentage + "%" ]); return ContentService .createTextOutput(JSON.stringify({ "result": "success" })) .setMimeType(ContentService.MimeType.JSON); } catch (err) { return ContentService .createTextOutput(JSON.stringify({ "result": "error", "error": err.toString() })) .setMimeType(ContentService.MimeType.JSON); } }
  7. Click Deploy → New deployment.
  8. Click the gear icon ⚙️ next to "Select type" and select Web app.
  9. Set the options:
    • Execute as: Me (your account)
    • Who has access: Anyone
  10. Click Deploy, authorize permissions, copy the Web App URL, and paste it into the teacher settings input on this page.
